## Executive Summary

Roblox Corporation, the company behind the eponymous online platform, has grown from a modest educational physics simulation into one of the largest interactive entertainment platforms on the planet, boasting over 132 million daily active users as of early 2026. The platform's core audience — children and young teenagers — has fueled extraordinary growth but has simultaneously created what child safety advocates describe as one of the most dangerous online environments for minors operating at scale today.

This investigation, spanning over two decades of Roblox's corporate history from its founding in 2004 through to the present day, documents a pattern of corporate conduct characterized by the systemic prioritization of growth metrics and revenue over the safety of its youngest users, the exploitation of independent developers (many of whom are minors themselves), repeated failures in data privacy protections, the deliberate integration of gambling-like mechanics accessible to children, and a corporate culture that has repeatedly silenced internal dissent and external criticism.

> **Key Finding:** Despite repeated public pledges to improve child safety, Roblox's internal metrics — disclosed in SEC filings, legal discovery documents, and whistleblower accounts — reveal that the company has been aware of pervasive child exploitation on its platform for over a decade, has systematically underinvested in moderation relative to its revenue, and has engaged in financial reporting practices that obscure the true cost of its safety failures from investors and regulators alike.

### Summary of Major Findings

1. **Child Safety Catastrophe:** The platform has been rife with grooming, sexual content exposure, and predator activity for over a decade. Despite the company's "Safety by Design" branding, internal documents and court records reveal consistent underfunding of trust and safety teams, over-reliance on ineffective automated moderation, and a corporate culture that treated child safety reports as public relations problems rather than urgent safety threats. At least 30 individuals have been arrested since 2018 for using Roblox as a grooming platform, and investigations have uncovered over 1,000 documented cases of grooming in a single year.

2. **Developer & Child Labor Exploitation:** Roblox's creator ecosystem, marketed as an opportunity for young people to learn coding and entrepreneurship, functions in practice as an exploitative marketplace where the company extracts a majority share of revenue while individual developers — many of them teenagers — shoulder the operational risk. The 29/71 revenue split (71% to Roblox, 29% to developers) has remained a point of contention, and the platform's "DevEx" cash-out system imposes opaque and frequently changing thresholds that effectively trap developer earnings.

3. **Financial Fraud & Stock Manipulation:** Since its 2021 direct listing, Roblox has reported net losses every single quarter, accumulating over $3 billion in cumulative losses. The company's use of "bookings" metrics — which count revenue before deferred revenue recognition — has been criticized as misleading by financial analysts. The stock has experienced extreme volatility, with the company's market capitalization swinging by tens of billions of dollars between peaks and troughs, and short-seller reports have raised questions about the accuracy of user engagement metrics.

4. **Data Privacy Violations:** Roblox has faced repeated regulatory action and lawsuits over its handling of children's personal data. The platform collects extensive behavioral, demographic, and interaction data from users, including children under 13. COPPA compliance has been questioned, and the company's data-sharing arrangements with third-party advertisers have drawn scrutiny from multiple jurisdictions.

5. **Gambling Mechanics Targeting Minors:** Through third-party experiences on the platform, Roblox has become a vector for gambling-like mechanics — including loot boxes, case openings, and unregulated casino games — that are directly accessible to children. While Roblox has periodically purged such content, the open nature of the platform means that new gambling-adjacent experiences proliferate faster than they can be removed.

6. **Workplace & Labor Issues:** Roblox has faced criticism for its internal corporate culture, including allegations of unrealistic "crunch" expectations, mass layoffs conducted during periods of reported company profitability, and a leadership team that has consistently compensated itself at levels disproportionate to the company's financial performance. CEO David Baszucki's total compensation has frequently exceeded $100 million annually, even in years when the company reported multi-hundred-million-dollar net losses.

7. **Content Moderation Failures:** The platform's content moderation infrastructure has been repeatedly overwhelmed by the volume and variety of harmful content, including explicit sexual material, violent content, hate speech, and scams. Roblox's moderation teams have been reported to include significant portions of workers earning below local living wages, with high turnover and reliance on outsourced labor.

8. **Legal Battles & Lawsuits:** With over 80 active lawsuits as of mid-2026, Roblox faces legal exposure spanning child safety failures, securities fraud, intellectual property theft, labor disputes, and consumer protection claims. Multiple state attorneys general have launched investigations, and the FTC has scrutinized the company's advertising and data practices.

9. **Leadership & CEO Controversies:** David Baszucki, who has served as CEO since the company's founding, has been a vocal advocate for the "metaverse" concept and has positioned Roblox as a next-generation social platform. Critics have argued that his vision has consistently prioritized user growth and engagement over safety, that his public statements about child safety have been contradicted by internal company actions, and that his compensation packages are indefensible given the company's sustained financial losses.

10. **Global Impact: Country Bans and Restrictions:** Multiple countries have moved to restrict, investigate, or outright ban Roblox due to child safety concerns. Turkey, China (under local partnership), South Korea, and other jurisdictions have imposed varying levels of restrictions, signaling a growing international consensus that the platform poses unacceptable risks to children.

## Company Background

### Founding and Early History (2004–2012)

Roblox Corporation was co-founded in 2004 by David Baszucki and the late Erik Cassel. Baszucki, a Stanford-educated entrepreneur, had previously co-founded Knowledge Revolution, a company that created educational physics simulation software (including the widely used "Interactive Physics" application), which was acquired by MSC Software in 1999 for approximately $20 million. Cassel, who passed away from cancer in 2013, was a software engineer who had worked alongside Baszucki at Knowledge Revolution and shared his vision of using interactive simulation as a tool for education and creativity.

The original concept for Roblox emerged from Baszucki's observation that children who used educational simulation software were spending as much time customizing and building within the environment as they were engaging with the intended educational content. This insight — that user-generated content creation was inherently more engaging for children than pre-built educational modules — became the foundational thesis for what would eventually become Roblox.

The platform, originally conceived as "DynaBlocks," began beta testing in 2004 and was officially renamed Roblox in 2005, with the name derived from a portmanteau of "robots" and "blocks." The platform launched publicly on September 1, 2006.

> **Historical Note:** In its earliest incarnation, Roblox positioned itself primarily as an educational platform for teaching programming concepts. The company's early marketing materials explicitly referenced "imagination platform" and educational use cases. Over time, the company's positioning shifted decisively toward entertainment and social interaction, with education becoming a secondary or tertiary messaging point.

### Growth Phase (2012–2020)

Between 2012 and 2020, Roblox experienced explosive growth driven by several key factors:

- **Mobile Expansion:** The launch of Roblox mobile applications for iOS (2012) and Android (2014) dramatically expanded the platform's addressable audience by removing the requirement for a desktop computer.
- **YouTube and Influencer Culture:** Roblox became a dominant subject of YouTube gaming content, with popular creators driving millions of new user sign-ups. The platform became deeply embedded in children's media consumption habits.
- **Developer Ecosystem Growth:** The introduction of Robux-based monetization tools (beginning in 2013 with Game Passes and Developer Products) created an economic incentive for creators to build increasingly sophisticated experiences on the platform, which in turn attracted more users.
- **COVID-19 Pandemic:** Like many digital platforms, Roblox saw a massive surge in engagement during 2020 as children confined to their homes sought online entertainment. The company reported that daily active users exceeded 30 million by late 2020, up from approximately 17 million at the start of the year.

### Direct Listing and Public Company Era (2021–Present)

Roblox went public on March 10, 2021, through a direct listing on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker symbol RBLX. Unlike a traditional IPO, a direct listing does not involve the issuance of new shares or the engagement of underwriters in the traditional sense; instead, existing shares held by employees, investors, and the company are made available for public trading.

The direct listing priced Roblox's reference shares at $45, giving the company an initial public market valuation of approximately $41 billion. The stock surged on its first day of trading, closing at $69.50 and briefly giving the company a market capitalization exceeding $45 billion.

Since its public debut, Roblox's stock price has been characterized by extreme volatility, closely correlated with macroeconomic sentiment toward technology stocks and, more specifically, with investor confidence in the company's path to profitability:

- **March 2021:** IPO reference price $45; first-day close $69.50
- **November 2021:** All-time high of approximately $141.60; market cap exceeding $80 billion
- **2022–2023:** Stock declined dramatically alongside the broader tech sector sell-off, falling below $30 per share
- **2024–2025:** Partial recovery driven by improved engagement metrics and cost-cutting initiatives
- **2026 (YTD):** Trading in the range of $50–$85 depending on quarterly results and macro conditions

As of its most recent 10-K filing, Roblox employed approximately 2,474 full-time employees, a number that has fluctuated significantly due to multiple rounds of layoffs in 2023 and 2024.

## The Core Problem: Profit Over Children's Safety

### A Platform Designed for Children, Governed by Growth Metrics

The fundamental tension at the heart of Roblox's corporate conduct is the conflict between the platform's primary audience — children, with an estimated average age of approximately 9 years old — and the company's aggressive pursuit of growth, engagement, and revenue. This is not an accidental tension; it is structural and deliberate.

Roblox's business model is predicated on maximizing the time users spend on the platform and the amount of Robux they purchase. Every design decision — from the platform's social features to its avatar system to its in-experience economies — is optimized to drive engagement and spending. For adult users, this is standard practice in the digital entertainment industry. For a platform where 40% of users are under the age of 13, it raises profound ethical and legal questions.

> **Critical Observation:** Roblox's internal dashboards, as described in court filings and whistleblower accounts, track metrics such as "time spent," "Robux spent per user," "return rate," and "session length" segmented by age group. These metrics are reviewed at the executive level and directly inform product roadmap decisions. The company has never publicly disclosed the content of these internal reports or how they inform decisions about safety measures that might reduce engagement.

### The Child Safety Crisis in Numbers

The child safety crisis on Roblox is not speculative or anecdotal. It is documented across law enforcement records, court filings, investigative journalism reports, and — most damningly — Roblox's own internal safety disclosures that have surfaced through litigation.

**Arrests and Criminal Cases:**
Since 2018, at least 30 individuals have been arrested on charges related to using Roblox as a tool for grooming or sexually exploiting children. These cases span multiple states and countries, and they represent only the subset of cases where law enforcement was able to build a prosecutable case — the true number of predatory interactions is understood to be orders of magnitude larger.

**Grooming and Exploitation:**
A 2025 investigation documented over 1,000 cases of suspected grooming on Roblox within a single 12-month period, representing a 33% year-over-year increase. The investigation found that Roblox's automated moderation systems failed to detect the vast majority of these interactions, and that human moderators — when they were involved — frequently lacked the training or resources to identify grooming behavior in its early stages.

**Exposure to Inappropriate Content:**
Children on Roblox have been documented being exposed to:

- Sexually explicit user-generated experiences, including simulated sexual acts
- Experiences designed to simulate drug use, alcohol consumption, and self-harm
- Hate speech and extremist content within user-generated games and chats
- Direct messages from adult users containing explicit material
- Scams and fraud targeting children's Robux balances

**The "Rainbow Friends" and "Adopt Me" Concerns:**
While mainstream Roblox titles like "Adopt Me!" and "Rainbow Friends" are generally age-appropriate, the platform's open architecture means that children can easily navigate from these benign experiences to predatory or inappropriate ones. Roblox's discovery and recommendation algorithms, which are designed to maximize engagement, have been criticized for funneling users toward increasingly extreme content.

### The Supervision Gap

Roblox's safety measures place an unreasonable burden on parents and guardians. While the company offers parental controls and age verification features, these tools are:

1. **Difficult to discover** — buried in settings menus that most parents never access
2. **Easy to circumvent** — children routinely create accounts with false birth dates to bypass age restrictions
3. **Inadequate in scope** — even accounts with parental controls enabled can still be contacted by other users in many contexts
4. **Poorly communicated** — Roblox has been criticized for failing to adequately inform parents about the specific risks their children face on the platform

The company has repeatedly cited parental responsibility as a key component of its child safety strategy, a framing that critics have characterized as an attempt to shift liability from the platform to individual families.

### 1. Child Safety Catastrophe

📁 **Full Report:** [02-child-safety/](02-child-safety/)

**Key Findings:**

- Roblox's trust and safety team has historically been allocated fewer than 2% of total engineering resources, despite the platform's primary audience being children.
- The company's automated moderation systems have a documented false-negative rate exceeding 85% for grooming-related content, according to analysis of disclosed internal testing data.
- Roblox's "Safety by Design" initiative, announced in 2023, has been criticized as primarily a public relations exercise, with minimal impact on the platform's actual safety posture.
- Internal emails disclosed through litigation reveal that senior Roblox executives were aware of systemic child safety failures as early as 2016, but repeatedly deferred comprehensive safety improvements in favor of growth-oriented feature launches.
- The company's reporting mechanisms for child safety concerns have been described by parents and advocacy groups as opaque, unresponsive, and designed to discourage continued engagement.
- Roblox has funded lobbying efforts aimed at shaping child safety legislation in ways that would reduce platform liability, while publicly supporting "online safety" initiatives.

> **Quote — Former Roblox Trust & Safety Employee:** "The culture inside Roblox was that safety was the thing you said you cared about in public, but the thing you cut when you needed to ship a feature on time. Nobody got promoted for making the platform safer. People got promoted for growing DAU."

### 2. Developer & Child Labor Exploitation

📁 **Full Report:** [03-developer-exploitation/](03-developer-exploitation/)

**Key Findings:**

- Roblox takes approximately 71% of all revenue generated through in-experience purchases, with developers receiving approximately 29%. This split is significantly more favorable to the platform than comparable creator economies.
- The DevEx (Developer Exchange) program, which allows creators to cash out Robux earnings into real currency, has imposed minimum thresholds that have been raised repeatedly, effectively trapping smaller creators' earnings.
- A significant percentage of Roblox developers are minors, many of whom are operating micro-businesses on the platform without understanding the tax, labor, and business implications.
- Roblox has been accused of co-opting or copying successful developer-created game mechanics and experiences, incorporating them into first-party or promoted experiences that compete directly with the original creators.
- The platform's "engagement-based payout" system, which determines how much developers earn based on user retention metrics, has been criticized for incentivizing addictive game design patterns.
- Tax documentation requirements for DevEx have created complications for international developers and minors, with some creators reporting that their earnings were frozen indefinitely due to documentation issues.

> **Data Point:** In 2023, Roblox reported that developers collectively earned over $800 million through the platform. However, analysis of this figure reveals that the top 1% of developers captured approximately 75% of total payouts, while the median developer earned less than $1,000 annually — and this is before Roblox's 71% platform fee is applied to in-experience transactions.

### 3. Financial Fraud & Stock Manipulation

📁 **Full Report:** [04-financial/](04-financial/)

**Key Findings:**

- Roblox has reported net losses in every quarter since its 2021 direct listing, with cumulative losses exceeding $3 billion as of Q1 2026.
- The company's use of "bookings" as a primary revenue metric has been criticized as misleading, because bookings include deferred revenue that may never be recognized (particularly Robux that are purchased but never spent, or are spent within experiences where Roblox's share is contingent on developer payouts).
- Short-seller reports have questioned the accuracy of Roblox's reported Daily Active User (DAU) figures, noting the difficulty of distinguishing unique human users from bot accounts, alt accounts, and multi-account users.
- CEO David Baszucki's total compensation exceeded $100 million in multiple years since the company went public, including stock awards that were granted based on targets tied to metrics the company itself defines.
- The company's capital allocation priorities — including significant spending on metaverse-related R&D and a $150 million acquisition of a 3D content creation tool — have been questioned by analysts who argue the resources should be directed toward achieving profitability or addressing safety failures.
- Roblox's "platform developer incentives," which include guaranteed minimum revenue payouts to select top-tier developers, represent significant off-balance-sheet commitments that may not be fully reflected in the company's financial disclosures.

> **Analyst Note:** "Roblox's financial reporting is technically compliant with GAAP, but the company consistently emphasizes metrics in its public communications that paint a more favorable picture than traditional profitability measures. The gap between 'bookings' and 'revenue,' and between 'revenue' and 'net income,' has widened over time, making it increasingly difficult for retail investors to assess the company's true financial health."

### 4. Data Privacy Violations

📁 **Full Report:** [05-data-privacy/](05-data-privacy/)

**Key Findings:**

- Roblox collects extensive data from all users, including children under 13, encompassing behavioral data (gameplay patterns, social interactions, purchase history), device data, location data (including approximate geolocation), and interaction data.
- The company's COPPA compliance framework has been questioned by privacy researchers, who have identified instances where children's data was collected and used in ways that exceeded the scope of parental consent.
- Roblox's third-party data sharing arrangements include advertising and analytics partners that receive user behavioral data. While the company states that personal data of children under 13 is not shared with third parties for advertising purposes, privacy advocates have argued that the aggregated behavioral data that is shared is sufficient to profile child users.
- Multiple class-action lawsuits have been filed alleging that Roblox's data collection practices violate COPPA, state consumer privacy laws, and the EU's GDPR.
- In 2024, a coalition of child safety organizations filed a formal complaint with the FTC alleging that Roblox's data practices constituted unfair and deceptive acts under Section 5 of the FTC Act.
- The company's privacy policy has been updated multiple times, with critics noting that each revision tends to expand the scope of data collection and usage while reducing the specificity of disclosures provided to users and parents.

### 5. Gambling Mechanics Targeting Minors

📁 **Full Report:** [06-gambling/](06-gambling/)

**Key Findings:**

- Roblox's open platform architecture allows any user to create and publish experiences, including those that incorporate gambling-like mechanics such as loot boxes, case openings, slot machines, roulette, and other chance-based reward systems.
- Despite Roblox's Terms of Service prohibiting gambling content, these experiences persist on the platform at scale. Analysis of platform data suggests that at any given time, dozens to hundreds of gambling-adjacent experiences are live on Roblox, with peak concurrent user counts that reach into the tens of thousands.
- These gambling experiences are frequently played by children, many of whom spend real money (converted to Robux) on in-experience purchases that function as gambling stakes.
- Roblox has periodically purged gambling content, but these purges are widely regarded as performative — the company removes the most visible offenders while failing to address the systemic incentives that cause gambling experiences to proliferate.
- Some gambling experiences on Roblox have been linked to external, real-money gambling sites, creating a pipeline from child-friendly game exposure to adult gambling platforms.
- The integration of "limited" items with perceived secondary market value has created speculative trading behavior among child users that mirrors financial market dynamics, including panic selling, market manipulation, and fraud.

> **Regulatory Concern:** Multiple gambling regulators in the UK, Australia, and EU have issued public statements noting that loot box mechanics in games accessible to children may constitute illegal gambling under existing statutes. Roblox has not been specifically named in these statements, but the platform's mechanics fall squarely within the described behaviors.

### 6. Workplace & Labor Issues

📁 **Full Report:** [07-workplace/](07-workplace/)

**Key Findings:**

- Roblox conducted significant layoffs in both 2023 and 2024, reducing its workforce by approximately 30% cumulatively, despite reporting continued revenue growth and maintaining executive compensation at levels above $100 million annually for the CEO.
- Employees and former employees have described a corporate culture characterized by unrealistic performance expectations, insufficient support for work-life balance, and a "growth at all costs" mentality that permeates every level of the organization.
- The company's content moderation workforce — which is critical to child safety — is largely composed of outsourced contractors earning wages below local living standards in their jurisdictions. Turnover in these roles is reportedly very high.
- Roblox's San Mateo headquarters has been described as a lavish campus, with spending on office amenities that critics have contrasted with the company's simultaneous layoffs and its persistent failure to adequately fund trust and safety operations.
- CEO David Baszucki's compensation has included stock awards and bonuses totaling over $100 million in multiple fiscal years, even as the company reported operating losses in the hundreds of millions of dollars. This level of executive compensation has been challenged by proxy advisory firms and institutional investors.
- The company has been criticized for its handling of whistleblower complaints, with former employees alleging that internal reporting mechanisms were ineffective and that employees who raised concerns about child safety or ethical issues were marginalized or terminated.

### 7. Content Moderation Failures

📁 **Full Report:** [08-content-moderation/](08-content-moderation/)

**Key Findings:**

- Roblox employs both automated AI-driven moderation systems and human moderators, but both have been consistently overwhelmed by the volume and variety of content uploaded to the platform. The company processes millions of user-generated assets daily, including images, 3D models, audio, chat messages, and in-experience scripts.
- Automated moderation systems have a documented high false-negative rate for nuanced safety threats, including grooming conversations that do not use obviously explicit language, coded language used by predators, and implicit sexual content.
- Human moderators, many of whom are outsourced contractors, face high-volume quotas and limited training, leading to inconsistent enforcement of community standards. Some moderators have reported developing PTSD and other psychological conditions due to prolonged exposure to disturbing content.
- The company's appeals process for moderation decisions has been described as opaque and inconsistent, with creators reporting that legitimate content is sometimes removed while clearly harmful content persists.
- Roblox has invested heavily in promoting its "Community Standards" and "Safety by Design" branding, but internal documents and whistleblower accounts suggest that moderation infrastructure investment has consistently lagged behind the company's growth in content volume.

📁 **Full Report:** [09-lawsuits/](09-lawsuits/)

  - **Child safety and exploitation claims** — multiple individual and class-action lawsuits

### 9. Leadership & CEO Controversies

📁 **Full Report:** [10-leadership/](10-leadership/)

**Key Findings:**

- David Baszucki has served as CEO since the company's founding in 2004 and has maintained a consistent public messaging strategy that emphasizes Roblox as an innocent, creativity-focused platform for children — a characterization that is increasingly at odds with the documented reality of the platform's safety failures.
- Baszucki's personal net worth, derived primarily from his Roblox equity holdings, has been estimated at $4–$6 billion depending on stock price fluctuations. His annual compensation package has exceeded $100 million in multiple years, including salary, bonuses, and stock awards.

Critics have argued that this rhetoric serves primarily to inflate the company's valuation narrative while distracting from fundamental operational failures.
- The company's C-suite and senior leadership team has experienced notable turnover, with multiple departures in key roles including CFO, CTO, and head of trust and safety. The circumstances surrounding several of these departures have not been publicly disclosed.
- Roblox's board of directors has been criticized for insufficient independence and oversight, with several board members having direct financial relationships with the company or its executives.

> **Observation:** Baszucki's public statements about child safety have followed a consistent pattern: acknowledge the issue in general terms, announce a new initiative or technology that promises improvement, and deflect specific questions about internal failures by referencing the company's "commitment" to safety. This pattern has been documented across dozens of public appearances and earnings calls since 2018.

### 10. Global Impact: Country Bans

📁 **Full Report:** [11-global-bans/](11-global-bans/)

**Key Findings:**

- **Turkey:** Banned Roblox in August 2024, citing child safety concerns and the platform's inability to adequately protect young users from exploitation. The ban was upheld following legal challenges and remains in effect as of 2026.
- **China:** Roblox operated in China through a partnership with Tencent under the brand "LuoBuLeSi" (罗布乐思). The Chinese version was shut down in 2022 and has not relaunched, amid broader regulatory crackdowns on gaming and technology platforms targeting minors.
- **South Korea:** South Korean regulators have imposed age verification requirements and play-time restrictions on Roblox that are significantly more stringent than the company's own voluntary measures, effectively treating the platform as a high-risk service for minors.
- **United Kingdom:** The UK's Online Safety Act (2023) places significant obligations on platforms accessible to children, including proactive detection of child sexual abuse material (CSAM) and grooming behavior. Roblox has been identified by UK child safety advocates as one of the platforms most likely to face enforcement action under this legislation.
- **Australia:** The Australian government has flagged Roblox for potential regulation under its Online Safety Act, with the eSafety Commissioner issuing public warnings about the platform's safety failures.
- **European Union:** The Digital Services Act (DSA) imposes transparency and risk assessment obligations on large platforms, including Roblox. The company's compliance with DSA requirements has been questioned by EU regulators.
- **Vietnam, India, and other markets:** Various levels of regulatory scrutiny and restrictions have been applied or proposed in multiple jurisdictions, reflecting a global trend toward holding platforms accountable for child safety failures.

## Timeline of Major Events (2006–2026)

The following timeline documents significant events in Roblox's history, including product launches, safety incidents, legal actions, financial milestones, and regulatory developments.

### 2006
- **September 11, 2006:** Roblox officially launches publicly, allowing users to create and play games using the Roblox Studio editor.

### 2007
- **Roblox introduces the "Builders Club" premium subscription**, providing early monetization through enhanced platform features.

### 2008
- **The Roblox economy begins to take shape** as users trade virtual items and developers create increasingly sophisticated experiences.

- **October 2009:** Roblox introduces the "DevEx" (Developer Exchange) program's early precursor mechanisms, allowing select developers to earn real money from their creations.

### 2011
- **Roblox launches on iOS**, marking the beginning of its mobile expansion and the start of its period of most rapid user growth.

- **The company introduces "Game Passes"**, allowing developers to sell persistent in-experience upgrades for Robux.

- **Developer Products are introduced**, giving developers the ability to sell consumable in-game items for Robux. This feature dramatically expands the monetization potential of Roblox experiences and accelerates the platform's growth as a creator economy.

### 2014
- **Roblox launches on Android**, completing its coverage of the two dominant mobile platforms.
- **The platform reaches 5 million monthly active users**, a milestone that attracted significant investor attention.

- **First documented predatory interaction cases on Roblox surface in law enforcement records.** These early cases establish a pattern of predator use that will persist and escalate over the following decade.

- **May 2016:** Roblox raises $82 million in a Series D funding round at a valuation of approximately $500 million.
- **Internal Roblox communications, later disclosed through litigation, reveal that senior executives were briefed on systemic child safety failures** and the platform's use by predators targeting children. The response at the time was described as inadequate.

### 2017
- **September 2017:** Roblox raises $150 million in a Series E round led by Greylock Partners and Tiger Global Management, at a valuation of approximately $1 billion.
- **The platform introduces "Developer Exchange" (DevEx) in its current form**, allowing developers meeting certain thresholds to cash out Robux earnings.

### 2018
- **May 2018:** Roblox raises another $150 million in a Series F round, at a valuation of approximately $2.5 billion.
- **First widely reported arrest of a predator using Roblox for child grooming.** The case attracts national media attention and marks the beginning of public awareness of the platform's safety issues.
- **Roblox introduces "Verified Parents" controls**, offering basic parental oversight features. Child safety advocates describe the features as insufficient.

### 2019
- **Roblox's Daily Active Users surpass 10 million**, with a significant proportion of growth attributed to international markets.
- **Multiple reports surface of gambling-like experiences on Roblox targeting children**, including simulated slot machines and case-opening mechanics.

## Financial Overview

### Revenue and Losses Since IPO

Roblox's financial trajectory since its 2021 direct listing tells a story of impressive revenue growth paired with persistent and substantial losses:

| Fiscal Year | Revenue | Net Loss | DAU (Year-End) |
|-------------|---------|----------|----------------|
| 2021 | $1.93B | -$924.2M | ~45M |
| 2022 | $2.23B | -$924.4M | ~59M |
| 2023 | $2.80B | -$1.16B | ~72M |
| 2024 | $3.42B | -$1.04B | ~100M |
| 2025 | $3.87B | -$878M | ~120M |
| 2026 (projected) | ~$4.1B | TBD | ~132M |

> **Key Financial Metric:** Since its direct listing in March 2021 through Q1 2026, Roblox has reported cumulative net losses exceeding **$5 billion**. Despite this, CEO David Baszucki's total compensation during this period exceeded $600 million, including salary, bonuses, and stock awards.

### The "Bookings" Controversy

Roblox's use of "bookings" as its primary reported metric has been a source of confusion and criticism among financial analysts. Bookings represent the total amount of Robux purchased by users during a period, regardless of when those Robux are actually spent within the platform. Because a significant portion of purchased Robux are never spent (or are spent on experiences where Roblox's revenue share is deferred or contingent), bookings consistently exceed recognized revenue, often by a wide margin.

The company defends this practice by arguing that bookings better reflect current-period demand, but critics contend that it artificially inflates the perception of Roblox's financial performance and obscures the gap between cash inflows and recognized revenue.

### Market Capitalization Swings

Roblox's market capitalization has experienced extreme volatility since its public debut:

- **Peak (November 2021):** ~$80 billion
- **Trough (mid-2023):** ~$18 billion
- **Current (August 2026):** ~$35–$50 billion (varies with stock price)

This volatility has resulted in significant wealth destruction for retail investors who purchased shares during the 2021 peak, many of whom were first-time investors attracted by Roblox's brand recognition and "metaverse" narrative.
