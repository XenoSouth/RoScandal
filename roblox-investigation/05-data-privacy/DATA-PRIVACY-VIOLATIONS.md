## Executive Summary

Roblox Corporation, one of the world's largest gaming platforms with over 70 million daily active users, faces mounting scrutiny over its data privacy practices—particularly regarding the collection, storage, and monetization of children's personal information. As a platform where approximately half of all users are under the age of 13, Roblox occupies a uniquely sensitive position in the digital landscape: it is simultaneously a playground for children and a data-driven advertising business.

This report examines the full scope of Roblox's data privacy violations, from noncompliance with the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) to the opaque handling of biometric data through third-party age verification systems. The findings reveal a pattern of expansive data collection that far exceeds what is disclosed in the company's privacy policy, a series of policy revisions that may amount to cosmetic changes rather than substantive reform, and significant risks posed by third-party data sharing arrangements.

The stakes are enormous. Under COPPA, penalties can reach $50,120 per violation. With tens of millions of children using the platform, the potential liability runs into the billions. More importantly, the long-term consequences for the children whose data is collected, analyzed, and shared remain poorly understood and largely unregulated.

## COPPA Violations

### Overview of the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act

The Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) is a federal law enacted in 1998 that imposes requirements on operators of websites and online services directed at children under 13, or that have actual knowledge of collecting personal information from children under 13. The law requires:

- **Verifiable parental consent** before collecting personal information from children
- Clear and comprehensive **privacy policies** describing data practices
- Reasonable **data security** measures
- Parents' ability to **review and delete** their children's information
- Prohibition on conditioning a child's participation on providing **more information than reasonably necessary**

COPPA is enforced by the Federal Trade Commission (FTC), which has the authority to bring enforcement actions and impose civil penalties.

### Roblox's COPPA Noncompliance

Despite its massive child user base, Roblox has been subject to significant criticism—and regulatory action—over its COPPA compliance.

#### FTC Investigation

The FTC investigated Roblox for collecting **geolocation data, chat logs, behavioral data, and device identifiers** from millions of children under the age of 13. This investigation revealed a stark disconnect between what Roblox's privacy policy claimed and what the platform actually did in practice.

Roblox's privacy policy has historically stated that it only collects **limited information** from users under 13:

- Username
- Password
- Date of birth
- Optional gender

However, the FTC's findings indicated that Roblox's actual data collection practices were far more expansive. The platform collected:

- **Geolocation data** — potentially revealing a child's physical location
- **Chat logs** — including communications between children and strangers
- **Behavioral data** — browsing patterns, in-game actions, spending habits, and engagement metrics
- **Device identifiers** — persistent identifiers that enable tracking across sessions and devices

This discrepancy between stated policy and actual practice is a hallmark of COPPA noncompliance. The FTC has consistently held that privacy policies must accurately describe data collection practices, not merely list a minimal set of data points while collecting far more.

#### The Penalties at Stake

Under COPPA, the FTC can impose penalties of **$50,120 per violation** as of 2023 adjustments. Given that Roblox has had tens of millions of child users, the theoretical maximum penalty is staggering. However, FTC settlements typically involve negotiated amounts far below the statutory maximum.

#### FTC Precedent: Recent COPPA Enforcement Actions

The FTC has been increasingly aggressive in COPPA enforcement in recent years, setting a clear trajectory toward harsher penalties:

| Company | Year | Fine | Key Violation |
|---------|------|------|---------------|
| **Epic Games** (Fortnite) | 2022 | **$520 million** | COPPA violations and dark patterns |
| **Microsoft** (Mojang) | 2023 | **$20 million** | Failure to obtain parental consent |
| **Disney** | 2023 | **$10 million** | Data collection without consent on child-directed content |
| **HoYoverse** (Genshin Impact) | 2024 | **$20 million** | Violating children's privacy rules |

The Epic Games settlement is particularly instructive. Epic was fined $520 million—comprising a $275 million COPPA penalty and $245 million for dark patterns—for practices remarkably similar to those alleged against Roblox: collecting children's data without consent and exposing children to potentially harmful communications.

This trajectory suggests that any future FTC action against Roblox would likely result in a settlement in the hundreds of millions, if not billions, of dollars—particularly given Roblox's larger child user base compared to most of the companies listed above.

## Data Collection Practices

### What Roblox Actually Collects

Roblox's data collection extends far beyond the limited information disclosed in its child-facing privacy policy. A comprehensive analysis of the platform's data practices reveals extensive collection across multiple categories:

#### Core Data Points

- **Geolocation data** — GPS coordinates, IP-derived location data, and regional identifiers
- **Chat logs** — Full transcripts of text and potentially voice communications
- **Behavioral data** — In-game actions, browsing patterns, time spent in experiences, interaction patterns
- **Device identifiers** — Hardware IDs, advertising IDs, session tokens, and persistent device fingerprints
- **Purchase history** — Robux transactions, virtual item purchases, and spending patterns
- **Social graph data** — Friend lists, group memberships, and interaction networks

#### Inferred Data

Beyond directly collected data, Roblox also generates inferred data about its users:

- Interest profiles based on game choices and engagement patterns
- Behavioral predictions used for engagement optimization
- Demographic estimates that may supplement or contradict self-reported information
- Risk scores and behavioral classifications

### Third-Party Age Verification: The Persona System

In late 2025, Roblox introduced a third-party age verification system operated by **Persona**, a digital identity verification company. While framed as a safety measure, this system introduces significant new privacy risks.

#### What Persona Collects

The Persona age verification system collects:

- **Facial scans** — Biometric data derived from users' faces
- **Government-issued identification** — Driver's licenses, passports, and other official documents
- **Biometric templates** — Mathematical representations of facial features used for matching

#### Persona's Data Retention and Use

Perhaps most concerning are Persona's data retention and usage practices:

- **Three-year storage** — Biometric data is retained for up to **three years**, far longer than what most users or parents would expect
- **Watchlist comparison** — Facial scans are compared against **watchlists**, raising questions about who is on these lists and for what purpose
- **Redundant data collection** — The system collects **redundant information**, potentially storing the same data through multiple means
- **Third-party processing** — Children's most sensitive biometric data is processed by a company that is neither Roblox nor the child's family

This means that children's facial biometrics—arguably the most sensitive category of personal data—are being collected, stored for years, and used for purposes that extend beyond simple age verification, all through a third party whose privacy practices most parents have never reviewed.

### Advertising Data and Third-Party Sharing

Roblox's privacy policy explicitly acknowledges sharing data with:

- **Business partners** — A broad category that could include virtually any commercial entity
- **Service providers** — Third-party companies that process data on Roblox's behalf
- **Advertising companies** — Data is shared with third-party ad networks and platforms

Under the broad definition of "sale" in the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA), certain data sharing practices may constitute a **sale of children's data**. The CCPA defines "sale" broadly as "selling, renting, releasing, disclosing, disseminating, making available, transferring, or otherwise communicating orally, in writing, or by electronic or other means, a consumer's personal information by the business to a third party for monetary or other valuable consideration."

Given that Roblox shares user data with advertising partners—and derives advertising revenue from this practice—the sharing of children's behavioral data may constitute a sale under CCPA, which carries additional requirements and penalties.

## Privacy Policy Changes

### Timeline of Policy Modifications

Roblox has made several notable changes to its privacy policy in recent years, particularly in response to increased regulatory scrutiny and public pressure.

#### May 2026: Advertising Restrictions for Minors

In May 2026, Roblox updated its privacy policy to **restrict personalized advertising for users under 18**. This change represented a significant shift from previous practice, where behavioral data from minors was used to serve targeted advertisements.

However, the practical impact of this change remains questionable:

- The restriction applies only to **personalized** advertising, not advertising in general
- Roblox may still collect behavioral data from minors for other purposes
- The definition of "personalized" may allow for significant data-driven targeting to continue
- Enforcement of this restriction is difficult to verify externally

#### March 2026: Parental Email Clarification

In March 2026, Roblox clarified that **parent email addresses** constitute the only category of COPPA-covered personal information collected from children under 13. This clarification was notable for its narrowness—it essentially confirmed that Roblox's official position is that the only regulated personal information it collects from children is the parent's email address used for consent.

This position is difficult to reconcile with the FTC's findings regarding extensive behavioral and device data collection.

#### The Pattern of Cosmetic Reform

Roblox's privacy policy changes follow a pattern common in the technology industry: **cosmetic revisions that address the most visible criticisms without fundamentally altering data practices**. Key indicators of this pattern include:

- Changes that address **specific allegations** rather than underlying practices
- Narrowing of **disclosed** collection without reducing actual collection
- Introduction of new categories of data use that offset restrictions on previous categories
- Policy language that becomes more precise without becoming more protective

Previous versions of the privacy policy contained more **ambiguous language** about the use of data for advertising, which may have been intentional to allow maximum flexibility in data monetization.

## Children's Data at Risk

### The Scale of the Problem

Roblox's child user base is enormous:

- **75 million+ users under 13** — approximately half of all daily active users
- The platform is explicitly designed to appeal to children through its game-like interface and social features
- Many users join at ages 8-10, spending years on the platform through their formative years
- The sheer volume of child users means that even small percentages of data misuse affect millions of children

### How Children's Data Is Used

#### Engagement Optimization

Children's behavioral data is used to optimize engagement—a practice that raises significant ethical concerns:

- Data informs **game design decisions** that maximize time spent on the platform
- Behavioral patterns are analyzed to identify **addictive loops** and engagement triggers
- In-game economies are calibrated based on children's spending patterns
- Content recommendation algorithms use behavioral data to keep children engaged

#### Advertising Strategy

Children's data informs broader advertising strategies:

- **Audience profiling** — Understanding the demographic and behavioral characteristics of child users
- **Advertiser targeting** — Using aggregated child data to attract advertisers seeking to reach young audiences
- **Market research** — Leveraging children's preferences and behaviors for commercial insight
- **Product development** — Using child data to inform the creation of new virtual items and experiences

#### The BBC Investigation

A BBC investigation into Roblox's age verification practices revealed **inadequate safeguards**:

- Accounts can be created with **fabricated birth dates** with no verification
- The platform's age-gating mechanisms are easily circumvented
- Young children routinely access content intended for older users
- Parental controls are insufficient and poorly communicated

This investigation demonstrated that Roblox's age-based privacy protections are undermined by the platform's failure to verify user ages accurately.

### The Fundamental Problem

The core issue is one of **informed consent**. Children under 13 cannot legally consent to data collection, and parents often lack the technical understanding to evaluate the scope of data being collected from their children. Roblox's privacy policy—while technically available to parents—uses language and concepts that are difficult for non-technical users to evaluate.

Moreover, the platform's design encourages children to engage deeply with its features, generating vast quantities of behavioral data that the children (and often their parents) are unaware is being collected.

## Third-Party Risks

### Data Sharing with Advertisers

Roblox shares user data with advertising partners, creating a chain of data custody that extends well beyond the platform itself:

- **Ad network partners** receive behavioral data to serve targeted advertisements
- **Measurement providers** receive data to evaluate advertising effectiveness
- **Analytics companies** receive data to provide insights to Roblox and its partners

Each of these transfers represents a point at which children's data may be exposed to additional parties, each with their own data retention and sharing practices.

### Third-Party Tracking

Beyond advertising, Roblox's platform includes various forms of third-party tracking:

- **SDKs and pixels** embedded in experiences may collect data independently
- **Social media integrations** may facilitate cross-platform data sharing
- **Analytics tools** provide third-party companies with access to user behavior data

### Cross-Platform Data Sharing

Roblox data may be combined with data from other platforms and services:

- Users may link Roblox accounts to **social media profiles**, enabling data combination
- Advertising networks may use Roblox behavioral data alongside data from other platforms
- Device-level tracking enables cross-platform profiling

### The Persona Risk (Revisited)

The third-party age verification tool operated by Persona represents a particularly acute third-party risk:

- A child's **facial biometrics** are shared with a company most parents have never heard of
- The data is stored for **three years**—during which the child grows and their face changes
- The data may be used for purposes beyond age verification, including **watchlist matching**
- If Persona experiences a data breach, children's biometric data could be exposed permanently
- Unlike passwords, biometrics **cannot be changed** if compromised

## International Privacy Concerns

### European Union: GDPR Implications

The European Union's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) imposes strict requirements on the processing of children's personal data:

- **Article 8** requires parental consent for processing children's data, with the age threshold set by individual member states (ranging from 13 to 16)
- Children's data receives **enhanced protection** under GDPR, requiring a lawful basis for processing
- The GDPR's **data minimization** principle requires that only necessary data be collected
- **Data Protection Impact Assessments** are required for high-risk processing activities

Roblox's expansive data collection practices may violate multiple GDPR provisions, particularly:

- Collection of **biometric data** through the Persona system, which is classified as a **special category** under Article 9
- **Behavioral profiling** of children, which may constitute automated decision-making under Article 22
- **Cross-border data transfers** to the United States, which require appropriate safeguards

#### European Economic Area (EEA) Child Accounts

Per Roblox's policy, information from EEA child accounts is **not used or shared without parental consent**. However, this policy faces several credibility challenges:

- Enforcement of this restriction is difficult to verify
- The definition of "parental consent" may not meet GDPR standards
- Data minimization questions remain unanswered
- The relationship between this policy and Persona's data collection is unclear

### Global Investigation Landscape

Multiple countries are investigating Roblox's data practices:

- **EU Data Protection Authorities** have examined Roblox's GDPR compliance
- **National regulators** in various countries have raised concerns about child safety and data practices
- **Consumer protection agencies** globally are scrutinizing platform data practices involving children

## The Age Verification Dilemma

### Roblox's AI Age Estimation System

In late 2025, Roblox introduced an **AI-powered age estimation system** as an alternative to traditional age verification. This system represents a fundamental tension between child safety and data privacy.

#### How It Works

- Users are asked to provide a **facial scan** or **government-issued ID**
- AI algorithms analyze the biometric data to estimate the user's age
- The estimated age determines which content and features the user can access
- For users under 18, additional restrictions are applied based on the estimated age

#### The Privacy Concerns

Privacy advocates have raised serious concerns about this system:

**Biometric Data Collection from Children**
- Children's faces are scanned and converted into mathematical representations
- These biometric templates are among the most sensitive categories of personal data
- Children cannot meaningfully consent to the collection of their facial biometrics

**Third-Party Storage**
- The biometric data is processed and stored by **third-party companies** like Persona
- Parents may not be aware that their child's facial data is being held by an external company
- The security practices of these third-party processors may not meet parental expectations

**Retained for Three Years**
- Facial biometric data is stored for up to **three years**
- During this period, children's faces change significantly as they grow
- The long-term use of this stored biometric data is unclear
- There is no guarantee that the data will be securely deleted after three years

**Watchlist Comparison**
- Perhaps most controversially, facial scans are compared against **watchlists**
- The composition of these watchlists is not publicly disclosed
- Children's faces are being run against databases of persons of interest without parental knowledge or consent
- The implications of a "match" for a child are not clearly described

### The Safety-Privacy Trade-Off

The age verification dilemma represents one of the most difficult challenges in children's digital policy:

#### Arguments for Age Verification
- Prevents children from accessing inappropriate content
- Enables age-appropriate safety measures
- May reduce exposure to predatory behavior
- Helps enforce platform rules more effectively

#### Arguments Against Current Implementation
- Biometric data collection is disproportionate to the safety goal
- Less invasive alternatives exist (e.g., device-based parental controls)
- Third-party data processing introduces unacceptable risks
- The scope of data collection exceeds what is necessary for age verification
- Children's biometric data should not be stored in commercial databases

#### The Fundamental Question

The age verification system raises a question that Roblox has not adequately answered: **Is the collection and long-term storage of children's facial biometrics a proportionate response to the age verification problem?**

Given that Roblox already collects date of birth from all users—and that the BBC investigation demonstrated the platform's age-gating is easily circumvented regardless—it is unclear whether biometric age verification actually improves child safety in practice, while the privacy costs are well-documented and severe.

## Conclusion

Roblox's data privacy practices represent a systemic failure to protect children's personal information. The platform collects far more data than its privacy policy discloses, shares this data with third parties whose practices are opaque to parents, and has introduced a biometric age verification system that subjects children to facial scanning and long-term data retention by external companies.

The regulatory landscape is tightening. The FTC's recent enforcement actions against Epic Games, Disney, and other platforms signal that penalties for COPPA violations are entering the hundreds of millions of dollars. The GDPR's enhanced protections for children's data create additional liability in European markets. And public awareness of children's data privacy is growing.

Roblox's privacy policy changes—while presented as reforms—appear to address specific criticisms without fundamentally altering the company's data-driven business model. The May 2026 restriction on personalized advertising for minors and the March 2026 clarification about parental emails are narrow adjustments that leave the core data collection infrastructure intact.

The most concerning development is the Persona age verification system, which introduces a new category of risk—children's biometric data held by third parties for years—under the guise of child safety. This system epitomizes the paradox at the heart of Roblox's approach to data privacy: **the company collects more and more data about children in the name of protecting them, while doing less than is necessary to safeguard the data it already holds.**

For parents, regulators, and child advocates, the message is clear: Roblox's data practices require sustained scrutiny, and the gap between the company's stated policies and its actual behavior remains dangerously wide.
