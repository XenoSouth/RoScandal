## 1. Primary Civil Court Dockets

### A. *Jane Doe v. Roblox Corporation, Discord Inc., and Uber Technologies, Inc.*
* **Court / Case No.:** U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California (Case No. 3:2026-cv-03513, April 2026)
* **Causes of Action:** Negligent Product Design, Facilitation of Human Trafficking and Child Exploitation, Failure to Warn, California UCL § 17200.
* **The Tripartite Exploitation Chain:**
  1. **Roblox:** Adult predator identified a minor female plaintiff in an open social game and used direct chat to groom her.
  2. **Discord:** Predator migrated communications to Discord to bypass Roblox text filters and extracted the child's home geolocation.

* **Legal Significance:** Directly challenges Section 230 immunity by targeting **defective product design** (absence of minor parental notifications and inadequate transit age verification).

### B. *S.U. v. Roblox Corporation and Discord Inc.* (SMVLC Litigation)
* **Court / Counsel:** California Superior Court / Matthew P. Bergman (Social Media Victims Law Center)
* **Plaintiff:** S.U., a minor groomed beginning at age 10 via unmoderated in-game chat and Robux bribery.
* **Clinical Harm:** Documented clinical depression, chronic self-harm, and multiple emergency psychiatric hospitalizations following prolonged sexual coercion.

### C. *Colvin et al. v. Roblox Corporation* (Parental Gambling Testimonies)
* **Court / Judge:** U.S. District Court, N.D. Cal. (Case No. 23-cv-04146-VC, Judge Vince Chhabria)
* **Testimonies:** Parents documented how minor children lost thousands of dollars on third-party gambling sites (**Bloxflip**, **RBLXWild**) connected via Roblox's OAuth login, with Roblox extracting a **30% marketplace fee** on cashout transactions.
* **Holding:** Judge Chhabria denied Roblox's motions to dismiss negligence and UCL claims, establishing potential platform liability for facilitating minor gambling ecosystems.

* **Court:** U.S. District Court for the Western District of Wisconsin
* **Facts:** 31-year-old Bryan Rogers met a 14-year-old girl in Roblox, migrated communications to encrypted apps, and transported her across state lines from Tennessee to Madison, Wisconsin. In January 2019, police executed a search warrant and discovered the missing child hidden in a closet in Rogers's home. Rogers was convicted under federal kidnapping statutes.
